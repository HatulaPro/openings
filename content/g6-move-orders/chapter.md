1...g6 and ...Bg7 work against every first move, and the king's knight stays on g8 until White has shown his
set-up. That is the point of the move order: White cannot know in advance which structure is coming.
- **c4 and e4 together:** Black plays ...d6 and ...Nf6 and is in the King's Indian, whose chapters take over.
- **A quiet system** (the London, Nf3 with g3, the English with g3, the Réti and the rest): Black keeps Modern
  options open, with ...d6, a knight to d7 or c6, and the ...e5 or ...c5 break.

This chapter was built from the White moves that come up most often in real 3+2 games, wide rather than deep.
Most lines reach a covered chapter or end with the plan after a few moves. The sources are Lichess studies
of 43 to 5100 likes. Several of the London and English studies are written from White's side, so they show
what White wants and not always Black's best answer. The weak spots are listed in the last section.

## The rule: King's Indian against c4 and e4
moves: 1. d4 g6 2. c4 Bg7 3. Nc3 d6 4. e4 Nf6
arrows: Gf6e4 Ge8g8

After 2.c4 Bg7 3.Nc3 d6 4.e4 Nf6 the position is 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 g6 3.Nc3 Bg7 4.e4 d6. 5.Nf3 with 6.Be2 is the
Classical and 5.f3 the Saemisch, both in their own chapters. Before ...Nf6, ...d6 already stops e4-e5, so
the knight can wait. 4...e5 instead is the accelerated King's Indian, a second choice. With the knight on g8,
...f5 needs no preparation. 4.Nf3 Nf6 5.e4 reaches the Classical too.

## 5.Bd3: the knight lands on d4
moves: 1. d4 g6 2. c4 Bg7 3. Nc3 d6 4. e4 Nf6 5. Bd3 Nc6 6. Nge2 e5 7. d5 Nd4
arrows: Gc6d4 Ge8e4
squares: Yd4

With the bishop on d3 and the knight on e2, d4 has fewer defenders than in the Classical, so ...Nc6 and
...e5 hit it before Black castles. After d5 the knight jumps to d4, and c3 cannot drive it away because
White's own knight is there. When White trades it, ...exd4 gives a Benoni-type structure: the e-file and the
long diagonal belong to Black, and ...Re8, ...Nd7-e5 and ...c6 are the plans.

## The Averbakh: ...c5 and ...e6 against the pin
moves: 1. d4 g6 2. c4 Bg7 3. Nc3 d6 4. e4 Nf6 5. Be2 O-O 6. Bg5 c5 7. d5 e6 8. Qd2 exd5 9. exd5 Qb6
arrows: Gb6b2 Gc8f5
squares: Rg5

Bg5 pins the knight and stops ...e5 for now (6...e5? 7.dxe5 dxe5 8.Qxd8 Rxd8 9.Nd5 is the known trap).
Black hits d4 with ...c5 instead and opens the e-file with ...e6. After the exchange on d5 the queen goes to
b6 against b2, and ...Bf5 follows. The Averbakh and the Makogonov (5.h3) have a chapter planned. Here they
get only their first moves.

## The Fianchetto: pin, trade, close, then ...f5
moves: 1. d4 g6 2. c4 Bg7 3. Nc3 d6 4. Nf3 Nf6 5. g3 O-O 6. Bg2 Nc6 7. O-O Bg4 8. h3 Bxf3 9. Bxf3 Nd7 10. e3 e5 11. d5 Ne7 12. e4 f5
arrows: Gf7f5 Ge7f5

Against g3 with Nc3 and Nf3, ...Nc6 and ...Bg4 aim at d4. Black gives the bishop for the knight, puts the
other knight on d7 and plays ...e5. After d5 the knight goes to e7, and ...f5 opens the game against a king
whose light-squared bishop has gone to f3. Without Nc3 (2.c4 Bg7 3.Nf3 d6 4.g3), the study's Modern plan is
...Nc6, ...Bd7, ...Qc8 and ...Bh3 to trade the fianchetto bishop, then ...h5-h4. The source itself calls it
slightly better for White with accurate play. 4...Nf6 into the Fianchetto is the safe alternative.

## The London: ...d6, ...Nd7 and ...e5
moves: 1. d4 g6 2. Bf4 Bg7 3. Nf3 d6 4. e3 Nd7 5. h3 e5 6. Bh2
arrows: Gd7e5 Ge5f4
squares: Yh2

The London bishop wants to sit on f4 and look at c7 and d6. ...d6 and ...Nd7 prepare ...e5, which hits the
bishop and gains space. After h3 and Bh2 the bishop is out of play behind its own pawns. Black has two knight
routes: ...Nh6 with ...f5 and even ...g5, a kingside pawn storm against a king that has castled into it, or
...Ne7-f5 for a quieter game. The second option keeps ...Nf6 and answers c4 with ...Qe8 and ...Ne4. The elite
games prefer ...e5 there.

## The aggressive London: Nc3, e4, Qd2 and Bh6
moves: 1. d4 g6 2. Bf4 Bg7 3. Nc3 d6 4. e4 Nf6 5. Qd2 O-O 6. O-O-O Nc6 7. Nf3 Bg4
arrows: Rf4h6 Rd2h6 Gg4f3

With the knight on c3 and e4 played, the London becomes a 150 Attack: Qd2, long castling and Bh6 to trade
the bishop on g7. Black develops with ...Nc6 and ...Bg4 against d4. This line is a draft: the sources here are
written from White's side and give no clear improvement for Black.

## The English with g3: ...c5 against the Botvinnik
moves: 1. c4 g6 2. Nc3 Bg7 3. g3 d6 4. Bg2 Nf6 5. e4 O-O 6. Nge2 c5 7. O-O Nc6 8. d3 Rb8
arrows: Gc5d4 Gb7b5
squares: Yd4

Against c4, e4, g3 and Nge2 (the Botvinnik bind), Black plays ...c5 and ...Nc6 at once so that d4 never
becomes White's. Then ...Rb8 and ...a6 prepare ...b5. If White's knight comes to e2, ...Bg4 trades a defender
of d4. Without e4 (5.Nf3 O-O 6.O-O c5) the same plan applies. 1.Nf3 g6 2.c4 and 1.c4 g6 2.g3 reach these
positions too. 4...e5 instead is a reversed King's Indian, with ...a5 against White's b4.

## The Réti and the King's Indian Attack: a Closed Sicilian reversed
moves: 1. Nf3 g6 2. g3 Bg7 3. Bg2 d6 4. O-O Nf6 5. d3 O-O 6. e4 c5 7. Re1 Nc6 8. c3 e5
arrows: Gb7b5 Ge5d4

When White plays d3 and e4 without d4, Black takes the centre with ...c5, ...Nc6 and ...e5. The pawn on e5
takes d4 away from White, and once White plays a3 Black answers with ...b5. With d4 instead of d3 the
position is a King's Indian Fianchetto.

## Rare first moves
moves: 1. b4 g6 2. Bb2 Nf6
arrows: Rb2h8

**1.b4:** with the g-pawn gone, Bb2 hits the rook on h8, so 2...Bg7? 3.Bxg7 just loses a bishop. 2...Nf6
first, then ...Bg7, ...d6 and ...e5, and the bishop on b2 bites on granite. **1.f4:** the usual
...Bg7, ...Nf6 and ...O-O, then ...d6 and ...e5 or ...c5. **1.g3 and 1.e3:** the sources give ...Nf6,
...O-O and a central break. 1.g3 is given with ...d5, not ...d6.

## What is still a draft
- **5.Bd3** is sourced only to 7...Nd4, from a course outline. 5.Bd3 O-O, the elite games' choice, has no
  source line. **5.Bg5** has no source at all.
- **The London:** 4.c3, 4.h3, 4.Nc3 and 5.h4 have no source line, though the plan (...Nd7, ...e5) is the same.
  **2.Bf4 Bg7 3.Nc3** is a draft.
- **The English:** nothing after 7...Nc6 in the quiet line, 6...e5 against the Botvinnik is not covered, and
  the reversed King's Indian rests on one White-side study.
- **The Levy plan** against 3.Nf3 d6 4.g3 rests on one study with 43 likes.
- **1.b4, 1.f4, 1.g3 and 1.e3** rest on one study each or on Wikibooks only.
- **Not covered:** 2.g3, 2.Bg5, 2.e3 (Colle), 2.Nc3 Bg7 3.Bg5, 2.h4.
