This chapter covers everything Black can play on move two except 2...Nf6, which has its own chapters. The weight is on 2...Nc6, the most popular reply and a straight copy of White's move. Against it the main line is 3.Bc4: it keeps one set of ideas for both 2...Nc6 and 2...Nf6, because the natural 3...Nf6 transposes into the sibling chapter on 3.Bc4, and every other third move runs into a queen raid on g4 or a quick f-pawn break. The Vienna Gambit 3.f4 is the second weapon, with its own lines for 3...exf4 and for the three ways of declining.

Against 2...Bc5 the main line is 3.f4 d6 4.Nf3, with the bishop pair won by Na4, and 3.Na4 is the quiet alternative. Against 2...Bb4 the main line is the direct 3.d4. The remaining second moves (2...d6, 2...f5, 2...g6, 2...c6) all fail to develop a piece, and White's answer is the same each time: strike with d4, take on d4 with the queen and pick up the tactics that follow.

The chapter is built from popular Lichess studies, mainly anomality's "Vienna Repertoire" and the studies by Bosburp, Chessmate_0204 and ST123_stsoham_Chess listed below. They disagree in places, for example on 2...Bc5 and 2...Bb4, and the notes say where. The studies stop where the plans take over, and the notes say so where a line is left without a verdict.

## The map: two roads against 2...Nc6
moves: 1. e4 e5 2. Nc3 Nc6
arrows: Gf1c4 Gf2f4 Gd1g4
squares: Yf7 Yg7

White has two good third moves and the choice is between a safer and a sharper game.

- **3.Bc4** aims at f7 and keeps the g1 knight at home so that the queen can reach g4 at once. Black's sensible answers are 3...Nf6 (transposes to the sibling chapter), 3...Bc5 4.Qg4, and the quiet 3...d6 4.a3 and 3...h6.
- **3.f4** is the Vienna Gambit against the copycat knight. After 3...exf4 4.Nf3 White gets a pawn centre with d4 and the pawn comes back with Bxf4. The three ways of declining (3...d6, 3...Nf6, 3...Bc5) are all covered.

Note that the stock reply to 3.Bc4, 3...Nf6, is not part of this chapter. Everything that follows 3...Nf6 4.d3 is in the sibling chapter "Vienna with 3.Bc4".

## The copycat 3...Bc5 and the Qg4 raid
moves: 1. e4 e5 2. Nc3 Nc6 3. Bc4 Bc5 4. Qg4
arrows: Gg4g7 Rc5f2 Gc4f7
squares: Rg7

Black copies White and puts the bishop on c5, but a bishop that leaves f8 stops guarding g7. The queen goes to g4 straight away and the pawn is attacked together with the rook on h8. This is why the knight stays on g1: Nf3 would sit on the queen's diagonal and spoil the idea.

Black has four tries and White has an answer ready for each.

- **4...g6** defends the pawn with a pawn and gives up the dark squares. White retreats 5.Qf3, threatening mate on f7, and builds Nge2, d3 and Qg3.
- **4...Kf8** keeps the pawn with the king and gives up castling. The plans are the same as against ...g6; the studies point to it as Black's best try.
- **4...Qf6** defends g7 and attacks f2, but 5.Nd5 gives the pawn for an attack: after 5...Qxf2+ 6.Kd1 the black queen is nearly trapped.
- **4...Nd4** takes the initiative, but 5.Qxg7 ignores the fork: if Black takes on c2, Qxf7 is mate.

The quiet 4.Nf3 is possible and equal. The point of 3.Bc4 is to play for more than that.

## The set-up against ...g6: Qf3, Nge2, d3
moves: 1. e4 e5 2. Nc3 Nc6 3. Bc4 Bc5 4. Qg4 g6 5. Qf3 Nf6 6. Nge2 d6 7. d3 Bg4 8. Qg3
arrows: Gg3e5 Gc1g5 Ge2d4 Rg4e2
squares: Yd4 Rf7

Once the queen is on f3 the pieces have fixed jobs.

- **The queen on f3** pins down f7 with the bishop, which forces Black to block with ...Nf6 at once. 5...Nd4 loses to Qxf7#.
- **The knight goes to e2, not f3.** From e2 it covers d4, so that ...Nd4 (which attacks the queen and c2) can be met by Nxd4, and it can go to g3 later. The pawn on d3 then frees the c1 bishop.
- **The bishop and the queen on the dark squares.** After ...Bg4 the queen steps to g3, from where Bg5, Qh4 and Nd5 pile up on the f6 knight. Black has holes on f6 and h6 and no safe home for the king: short castling walks into the dark-square weaknesses, long castling is stopped by the queen on h4 looking at d8.
- **Bosburp's move order** plays 7.h3 first to stop ...Bg4 and ...Ng4. After 7...Be6 8.d3 the capture ...Bxc4? loses to Bg5.

## The f2 pawn: sacrifices and king walks
moves: 1. e4 e5 2. Nc3 Nc6 3. Bc4 Bc5 4. Qg4 Qf6 5. Nd5 Qxf2+ 6. Kd1
arrows: Gg4g7 Gd5c7 Rf2d4
squares: Yd1 Rf8

The Qg4 lines are full of the same few tactics, and it is worth knowing them as patterns.

- **Qxf7 mate.** Queen and bishop both attack f7 and the king is the only defender. Any move that neither blocks the f-file nor defends f7 (...Nd4, ...Nge7) loses at once.
- **The poisoned f2 pawn.** After 4...Qf6 5.Nd5 Qxf2+ 6.Kd1 White has two threats, Qxg7 and Nxc7+, and Black has one move that deals with both: ...Kf8. Every other reply loses material. If Black plays 6...g6, Nh3 traps the queen on d4.
- **The ignored fork.** After 4...Nd4 White takes g7 and does not mind ...Nxc2+, because Qxf7 is mate. Queen and bishop beat a fork.
- **The king on d1.** The walk ...Qxf2+ Kd1 looks frightening, but Black's queen is alone and short of squares, and White's threats (Qxg7, Nxc7+) come first.

## The Vienna Gambit against 2...Nc6
moves: 1. e4 e5 2. Nc3 Nc6 3. f4 exf4 4. Nf3 Nf6 5. d4
arrows: Gd4c5 Gd4e5 Gc1f4 Rd8h4
squares: Yf4 Yd4

With 3.f4 White sacrifices a pawn for the centre, as in the King's Gambit but with the knight on c3 already.

- **4.Nf3** is the point: the knight stops ...Qh4+ and prepares d4. Black can only keep the pawn with ...g5.
- **5.d4 Bb4 6.Bd3** is the main structure. The bishop guards e4 so that the pin on c3 does nothing, Bxf4 regains the pawn with a gain of time, and White castles. Black's natural castling is a mistake because White simply plays Bxf4, O-O and answers ...Nxe4 with d5.
- **The pawn recapture on c3 is bxc3** after ...Bxc3+: the chain c3-d4-e4 stays solid and the b-file opens for the rook.
- **4...g5 5.Bc4 g4 6.O-O** is the speculative branch. White gives the knight on f3 for a Muzio-type attack on f4 and f7. It is playable and the study shows strong attacking ideas, but it is the least tested line in the chapter, and the same studies warn that the knight on f3 is in danger after ...g5.
- **4...d6, 4...Bb4 and 4...Bc5** are all met by 5.d4: the centre first, the pawn back with Bxf4, and an attack on the queen side or on the g-file.

## When Black declines the gambit
moves: 1. e4 e5 2. Nc3 Nc6 3. f4 d6 4. Nf3 Bg4 5. Bb5
arrows: Gb5c6 Rg4f3 Gf4e5
squares: Rc6

Over half the players at club level decline the pawn, and each of the three ways has a plan.

- **3...d6 4.Nf3 Bg4 5.Bb5.** The bishops pin each other's knights. Black plays ...a6, and after 6.Bxc6+ bxc6 7.fxe5 dxe5 8.h3 and recaptures on f3 with the queen. Black's pawns on c6, c7 and e5 are loose, and White has the f-file, d3 and the plan of long castling.
- **3...Nf6 4.fxe5 Nxe5 5.d4.** The Corell Variation. The knights are driven back by d4 and e5, and Black must find 7...d5! after 6.e5 Ng8 7.Nf3. Everything else loses a pawn or worse: 7...d6? 8.Bb5, 7...Bb4? 8.d5. The same position arises from 2...Nf6 3.f4 Nc6.
- **3...Bc5 4.Nf3 d6 5.Na4.** The Quelle Variation. The knight takes the bishop pair, the bishop on b5 cannot be kicked after Nxb6 axb6 because the a-pawn is no longer on a6, and f5 gains space.

## 2...Bc5: the bishop pair and the f-pawn
moves: 1. e4 e5 2. Nc3 Bc5 3. f4 d6 4. Nf3 Nf6 5. Bc4 O-O 6. d3 Bg4 7. Na4 Bb6 8. Nxb6 axb6
arrows: Ga4c5 Gf4f5 Gc1g5 Gd1h5
squares: Rb6 Yg4

With the bishop on c5 the f-pawn needs support. The bishop eyes f2 and g1, so 3.f4 is only safe if White covers g1 and h4 at once with 4.Nf3 and is ready to castle quickly. Two of the larger studies recommend 3.f4 here, and the biggest (anomality) gives it as the accepted Jeanisch Gambit next to its main chapter on 3.Na4.

- **Na4 for the bishop pair.** With no knight on c6 Black has no ...Nd4 resource, so White can spend time on Na4 and Nxb6, which gives him the bishop pair and doubles Black's b-pawns. This is the same idea as in the Quelle line of 2...Nc6.
- **f5.** The pawn blocks the c8-h3 diagonal, cages a bishop on c8 and prepares Bg5 and Qh5 or Qh3.
- **The king-side attack.** With the bishop pair and the pawns on f5 and e4, the plan is h3 (forcing the bishop to decide), Bxf3, Qxf3, Bg5, Qh3 or Qh4, and a rook lift Rf3-h3.
- **3.Na4** is the quiet alternative: the knight attacks the bishop at once, and after 3...Bb6 4.Nxb6 axb6 5.d4 the bishop pair and the structure are White's, with no risk.

## 2...Bb4: the quick centre
moves: 1. e4 e5 2. Nc3 Bb4 3. d4 Bxc3+ 4. bxc3 exd4 5. cxd4 d6 6. Bd3
arrows: Gg1e2 Gc2c3 Ga2a4 Gc1a3
squares: Yd4 Rb4

After 2...Bb4 White gives up no time and takes the centre with 3.d4. After ...Bxc3+ and bxc3 White has the bishop pair and the pawn centre, and the plan is plain: Bd3, Ne2, O-O and c3, then a4, Ba3 and f4 with the pieces aimed at the king side. The b-file is half open and Rab1 limits the black bishop.

- **3...exd4 4.Qxd4** Bxc3+ 5.Qxc3 Nf6 6.Be3: the e4 pawn is not hanging, because ...Nxe4 runs into Qxg7.
- **3...Nc6 4.dxe5 Nxe5 5.Qd4:** the queen forces ...Bxc3+ and after the right recapture after 7.Nf3 Nxf3+ is 8.gxf3 (not Qxf3), which opens the g-file.
- **3.f4!?** is a trap line (...Bxc3 bxc3 exf4 Qg4 Qf6 Ne2 g5? h4 wins). It is not the main line.

A warning. The app's book of elite games contains 133 games from this position and not one continues with 3.d4: they play 3.Bc4, 3.Nd5 (the Nikolic Variation, the main line of anomality's study), 3.Nf3 and 3.Nge2. The move comes from one study with 519 likes and a sideline of another, so it is the least tested choice in this chapter.

## Rare second moves: strike with d4
moves: 1. e4 e5 2. Nc3 c6 3. d4 d5 4. dxe5 dxe4 5. Qxd8+ Kxd8 6. Bg5+
arrows: Gd4e5 Gg5d8 Rc6c5
squares: Yd8 Rd5

Moves like 2...d6, 2...g6 and 2...c6 do not develop a piece, and the answer is always the same: 3.d4. Black either takes (3...exd4 4.Qxd4) and the queen develops with tempo, or he holds with ...d5 and loses the right to castle after the queen trade.

- **2...d6 3.d4** is the Cenni Variation: Qxd4, Qd3, Be3 and f4. The sharper 3.f4 exf4 4.Qf3 (the Omaha Gambit) is a second option, and 4...g5? 5.h4 f6? 6.Qh5+ wins material.
- **2...f5** is a Latvian Gambit without the f3 knight: 3.exf5 Nf6 4.g4 d5 5.g5 Ne4 6.Qh5+ Ke7 7.f6+ wins material.
- **2...g6 3.d4 exd4 4.Qxd4** attacks g7, and 4...Qf6 5.Qxf6 Nxf6 6.e5 Ng4 7.Nd5 wins material in the study's line. The study does not cover 4...Nc6.
- **2...c6 3.d4 d5 4.dxe5 dxe4 5.Qxd8+ Kxd8 6.Bg5+** leaves Black without castling and a pawn down. After 3...exd4 4.Qxd4 Nf6 5.e5 White wins time on the knight.
- **Typical tactics:** a knight jump to d5, e4 or d6 forking king and rook, a bishop on g5 that pins the queen to the king, and smothered mate on e8 after Nd6.
